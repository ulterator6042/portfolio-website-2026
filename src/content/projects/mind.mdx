<Quote>Interruptions are inevitable.</Quote>

<Section>
The average worker goes through 25 cycles of focus, interruption and re-entry in
a single day, losing the precious momentum they had built while focusing on a
task. Each cycle runs the same loop — focused work, an external trigger, the
interruption, its resolution, and a slow re-entry to rebuild lost context.
</Section>

<Section>
## Priorities according to users

When we asked what a recovery tool would need to earn a place in their workflow,
professionals ranked their priorities clearly:

1. Speed of interaction
2. Ease of resumption
3. Depth of detail capture
4. Ability to save multiple contexts
5. Control over what is saved
6. Privacy and data handling

<p style="font-family: var(--font-mono); font-size: 0.75rem; color: var(--muted);">*153 survey samples</p>
</Section>

<Quote>One trigger saves everything you were doing before an interruption; one trigger brings it all back.</Quote>

<Section>
It rebuilds your context and reminds you where you left off, so returning to work
costs seconds instead of minutes.
</Section>

<Section>
## The re-entry, with and without

Mapping the same six moments — from deep focus to resumption — shows where the
tool changes the experience: it removes the manual reconstruction and protects
the momentum that would otherwise drain away.
</Section>
